The SAT uses the discriminant in two ways.

First: how many real solutions does this equation have? Compute b squared minus 4ac, check the sign, answer.

Second: given a condition on the number of solutions, find the value of a constant. Set the discriminant equal to zero for exactly one solution, greater than zero for two solutions, or less than zero for no real solutions, then solve for the unknown.

Practice problem. The equation 2x squared plus kx plus 8 equals 0 has no real solutions. What values of k are possible?

No real solutions means b squared minus 4ac is less than zero. Here b is k, a is 2, c is 8. k squared minus 4 times 2 times 8 is less than zero. k squared minus 64 is less than zero. k squared is less than 64. So k is between negative 8 and positive 8, not including the endpoints.

That's a clean discriminant problem. Same setup every time: translate the solution condition into a discriminant inequality or equation, solve.
